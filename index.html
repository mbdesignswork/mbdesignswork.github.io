How it works

A clear process for each prototype stage.

You can start with a rough idea, an old board, a breadboard circuit, or a half-working prototype. The first goal is to understand the problem, pick the right technical path, and avoid wasted build time.

01

Send the details

Describe the intended use, what you need, your available files, budget, and target timing. Mention any confidentiality requirements before sharing sensitive files.

02

Agree the scope and terms

Confirm deliverables, testing limits, exclusions, price, and schedule. After proposal acceptance, agree a short services agreement before payment or work begins.

03

Approve and fund the work

Receive the payment link for the agreed service or hours. Work starts on the agreed schedule; additional hours, scope changes, and outside costs require approval.

04

Review the deliverables

Receive the agreed files, models, or technical notes, with completed checks, known limitations, and any further testing or revisions identified for the next stage.

Project fit and testing

Each project is assessed for its intended use, technical requirements, testing needs, and available support. Scope and scheduling are confirmed before acceptance. Prototype testing covers only the agreed checks and conditions.

Services do not include professional engineering seals, product certification, regulatory approval, or independent safety sign-offs. Flight-critical, life-support, and safety-critical vehicle-control work is outside the current service scope.

Rates & billing

Starting at CA$70/hr with prepaid project credits.

Project support for new clients starts at CA$70/hr. After the scope and services agreement are accepted, hourly work begins with a 2-hour prepaid minimum of CA$140. Time is deducted as work is completed; the initial credit is not a fixed price for completing an entire project.

Work pauses when the available credit is used. Further work requires approval and prepayment of a minimum 2-hour top-up at the agreed rate. Scope changes and additional costs are confirmed before they are incurred.

The credit policy is confirmed in the project agreement before payment: when a project finishes, unused credit of 2 hours or less stays on file for future work; more than 2 unused hours can be refunded on request or retained as credit. This policy is subject to any refund or cancellation rights required by applicable law.

Pricing covers design and support labor only. Components, PCB manufacturing, 3D print material, print time, outside fabrication, shipping, specialty modules, and replacement parts are separate unless clearly included in a written quote.

FAQ

Common questions before starting.

Straight answers about projects, hourly work, consultation, parts, CAD, 3D printing, firmware, and deliverables.

Who is behind MB Designs Work?

Michael Bradshaw runs MB Designs Work. He has studied Electrical Engineering & Management at McMaster University and has practical experience in electronics manufacturing support, PCB work, testing, debugging, CAD fixtures, 3D printing, and prototyping.

What kinds of projects do you take on?

Projects include consumer electronics, product prototypes, toy circuits, custom boards, sensor circuits, audio circuits, Bluetooth/IoT prototypes, motor-control hardware, breadboard-to-PCB work, PCB reviews, firmware bring-up, reverse engineering, test fixtures, enclosures, brackets, and 3D printed support parts.

How do prepaid credits and top-ups work?

Scope and the services agreement come first. Hourly work for new clients then starts with a CA$140 (2-hour) prepaid credit at CA$70/hr. Work pauses when credit is used and resumes after an approved top-up at the agreed rate. See Rates & billing for unused-credit and separate-cost terms.

Can you design the full PCB for me?

Full-board work is considered after the intended use, requirements, interfaces, layout complexity, and testing needs are clear. Scope, deliverables, and availability are agreed before a project is accepted. See the project scope for current service limits.

Do you do firmware and Bluetooth?

Yes, for bring-up and practical prototype support: simple microcontroller logic, sensor interfaces, BLE/Bluetooth modules, serial communication, motor-control logic, and hardware-firmware integration. Production firmware development and validation require a separately agreed scope and suitable resources.

Do you offer 3D printing?

Yes. CAD and 3D printing support can include enclosures, brackets, clips, PCB holders, test fixtures, programming fixtures, small printed parts, and STEP/STL files. Design labor is hourly, while print material, print time, and outside fabrication are quoted separately.

Can you reverse engineer an old board or circuit?

Yes. Work can include tracing connections, documenting wiring, identifying parts where possible, recreating schematics/PCBs, and planning a replacement or updated revision.

Do you work on existing products and prototypes?

Selected troubleshooting, documentation, CAD, and revision tasks can be considered. Send a short description of the application, operating conditions, and the help needed so suitability can be assessed before any work is accepted.

What should I send for a project request?

Start with the intended use, project goal, available information, budget, target timing, and the output you need. Include known power requirements and testing needs where relevant. Mention confidentiality requirements before sending sensitive design files.
