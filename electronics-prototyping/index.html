Build on what you already have

You do not need a finished specification to discuss a prototype. A sketch, breadboard, collection of development modules or existing board can be the starting point. The first step is to understand the intended use, the current behavior and the result you need.

Work can include product electronics, sensors, audio circuits, lighting, displays, buttons, motor-control hardware and microcontroller integration. A project may cover one defined problem or a sequence of design, build and test stages.

Bring-up and fault isolation

  • Power and wiring: continuity checks, power-rail measurements, connector checks and review of the intended power path.
  • Signal behavior: signal tracing, noise investigation and checks at relevant circuit interfaces.
  • Hardware integration: sensor connections, module interfaces and coordination between electronics and firmware.
  • Next-revision planning: circuit changes, component choices, wiring documentation and a practical test plan.

The available equipment, operating conditions and measurements are agreed for each task. Test notes distinguish observed behavior from suspected causes and identify checks that remain open.

From breadboard to a custom PCB

A working breadboard or module prototype can establish the basic function before a custom board is developed. The PCB phase needs its own decisions about connectors, power, physical size, component placement and fabrication.

Schematic and PCB design can be included as a separate phase. Embedded firmware and enclosures or fixtures can also be scoped where the prototype needs them.

Working together worldwide

Design reviews, troubleshooting discussions, files and progress updates can be handled remotely. If hands-on bench work is needed, access to the board, shipping, equipment and scheduling are agreed before work starts.

Send the intended function, a description of the fault, what you have already tried, available schematics or photos, power requirements and any measurements. Start with non-confidential information. Completed prototype checks apply to the tested conditions; product certification and release approval remain separate.
