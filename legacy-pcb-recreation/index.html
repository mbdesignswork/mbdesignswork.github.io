Understand the existing board first

An old board may come with missing files, unclear wiring or components that are difficult to source. The first task is to assess what information is available and what the replacement must preserve: connectors, mounting points, power requirements, interfaces and intended function.

Work can begin with a physical board, photographs, wiring notes or partial documentation. The condition of the hardware and access to a working reference affect what can be established.

What the work can include

  • Board and wiring documentation: connector identification, connection tracing and functional block notes.
  • Schematic recreation: organizing traced connections into circuit documentation with unresolved details identified.
  • Component assessment: identifying parts where possible and reviewing potential alternatives against the application.
  • PCB recreation or redesign: rebuilding design files or planning an updated board with agreed mechanical and electrical interfaces.
  • Build preparation: BOM checks, manufacturing outputs and notes for the replacement-board prototype.

A component that fits the same footprint is not automatically a suitable replacement. Electrical limits, operating conditions and the role of the part need to be checked before a substitution is accepted.

Recreation has practical limits

Visible board connections do not reveal every detail of a product. Hidden internal layers, unreadable markings, custom parts or unavailable firmware may leave important questions unresolved. Feasibility and the required investigation are assessed before promising a complete replacement.

The scope distinguishes documented connections, proposed design changes and behavior that still needs verification. A recreated board requires appropriate prototype testing; matching its appearance alone is not evidence of equivalent performance.

What to send for an assessment

Describe the equipment, why the board needs replacing, whether a working example is available and any known symptoms. Include non-confidential photos of both sides, connector information, approximate dimensions and the documentation you already have. Mention whether the goal is a direct replacement or a revised design.

Physical-board access, handling and shipping are agreed where needed. Related work includes PCB design and layout and prototype bring-up and testing.
